## Access caveats (observed on this host, not predicted)

- `ethiocta.gov.et` returned HTTP 503, and a Wayback snapshot of its homepage served a hijacked gambling-spam page. The domain is **not** cited and should be treated as compromised until re-checked. Ethiopian coffee instead cites a World Bank blog on EUDR exposure.
- `arfic.bi` resolves to a domain-for-sale page, not the Burundi coffee regulator, and is not used. Burundi coffee cites the World Bank project appraisal document.
- `afa.go.ke`, `coffee.agricultureauthority.go.ke` and `agricultureauthority.go.ke` (Kenya) timed out or failed DNS. Kenya coffee cites an AGRIS record from the Coffee Research Foundation.
- The FAO fisheries country-profile pattern (`/fishery/en/countryprofiles/<ISO>`) redirects to a soft 404 page. It was not cited; the affected fisheries themes were substituted with FAO non-wood forest product themes.
- `www.tropicultura.org` is a parked "awaiting validation" domain, so the Burundi coffee article hosted there was not cited.
- `teaboard.or.tz`, `coffeeboard.or.tz`, `coffeeboard.go.tz` and `tanzaniacoffee.org` failed DNS; Tanzania coffee remains omitted rather than guessed.
- `mauritiussugar.mu` returned 403; MCIA is used. `mexa.mu` failed DNS; `mexamauritius.org` is used.
- `fao.org/faolex/results/details/...` returned 403. The Djibouti salt decree PDF itself downloaded normally and was text-extracted, so it is cited directly.
- `ich.unesco.org` serves an anti-bot challenge to some fetchers; the barkcloth page was verified by direct HTTP request with a browser user agent (HTTP 200, full text).
- IFAD, AfDB, ITC and IITA (via the page fetcher) returned 403. IITA was verified by direct HTTP request; the page returns 200 with a browser user agent.
- PDFs cannot be read by the standard page fetcher. The Seychelles IOTC report, Comoros FAO profile, Djibouti decree, World Bank Burundi PAD, South Sudan CFSAM and UNDP South Sudan report were downloaded and text-extracted locally.
- The Seychelles SPGA annual report PDF extracted without the cinnamon section, so the cinnamon theme uses the FAO NWFP review instead.

## Source-quality flags for the coordinator

1. **Seychelles — four themes share one 2001 FAO review.** Cinnamon, coco de mer, breadfruit/santol and raffia/bamboo all cite the same Insular East Africa chapter. The cinnamon sales figure is from 1971 and is presented as historical. A current SPGA or National Bureau of Statistics source would strengthen these entries.
2. **South Sudan — four themes share one 2022 UNDP/Ministry report.** The report is a joint government-UN value-chain study, but it is not a product-level statistical source. It supports the selection of shea, gum arabic, sesame and groundnut as priority chains, not current volumes.
3. **Kenya — export manufacturing.** EPZA is cited for the EPZ programme and flagship zones, not for garments specifically. The theme label is "Export manufacturing" to avoid an unsupported garment claim.
4. **Mauritius — four themes cite MEXA.** Textiles, rum, seafood and jewellery all use MEXA's sector list. This mirrors the North Africa approach (ONAT used for several craft themes) but dedicated industry sources per product would strengthen each entry.
5. **Mauritius — tea.** The Bois Chéri citation is the estate operator's heritage page. It supports tea production and the visitor route; no area, volume or market-share claim is made.
6. **Madagascar — litchi.** The World Bank vanilla article mentions lychee only as freight crossing Route Nationale 5a. No volume or season claim is made.
7. **Comoros — ylang-ylang and cloves** share the 2019 AGRIS study; **vanilla and coconut** share the 2022 FAO profile. Both are institutional or peer-reviewed sources but neither gives current trade volumes beyond the figures quoted.
8. **Ethiopia — coffee.** The World Bank blog is a policy article about EUDR exposure, not a sector profile. A functioning ECTA or ECX source should replace it when available.
9. **Uganda — bananas.** IITA describes highland cooking bananas at regional (Eastern Africa) level; it is not a Uganda-specific statistical source. A NARO or UCDA source would be stronger.
10. **Djibouti — salt.** The citation is the national decree regulating Lake Assal salt, which establishes the legal regime; it does not report current output.

## Suggested sourcing upgrades

- Ethiopia: restored ECTA or Ethiopian Commodity Exchange page for coffee.
- Kenya: AFA Coffee Directorate or Nairobi Coffee Exchange page.
- Tanzania: Tanzania Coffee Board page for coffee, if the domain returns.
- Uganda: NARO or a Uganda-specific matoke/banana source.
- Seychelles: current SPGA forestry and cinnamon data; National Bureau of Statistics export series.
- South Sudan: FAO or UNDP product-level statistics for gum arabic, shea, sesame and groundnut.
- Comoros: a craft source for woven mats and embroidery, replacing the honey substitution if a strong source appears.
- Mauritius: a rum GI or chambers-of-agriculture page for rum; a fisheries division page for seafood.
- Burundi: a current ARFIC/ODECA source for coffee to supplement the 2016 World Bank project document.

## Notes on method

- Theme-level sources carry `title`, `url` and `accessedAt`. The root `sources` array carries `publisher`, `title` and `url` once per unique URL, in first-appearance order, matching the North and West Africa files.
- Every association sentence is limited to what the cited page or document states. Where a source is a category list, a legal text or a regional study rather than a product page, the sentence says so.
- No images, producer names, certifications or availability claims were added.
